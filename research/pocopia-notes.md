## 3. Core loop (important nuance)

Pokopia is **not an idle game** — it's an active cozy life-sim. You play a Ditto
transformed into a human, rebuilding a withered post-human Kanto: gather
materials, craft furniture, grow crops, restore habitats/biomes, befriend 300+
Pokémon, build homes. Build projects take real time (15 min to next day).
Supports 1–4 player co-op (local wireless + online) with shared town-building.

The "watch them live their lives" vibe the user loves comes from the **ambient
creature AI layered on top** of that loop — creatures behave autonomously in the
world you built. For our idle companion, that ambient layer IS the product.

## 4. How creatures express personality (the design gold)

**Ambient autonomous behavior.** Pokémon sleep, play with one another, forage
for food when hungry, and use their specialties unprompted — "accurately
determining when to use their specialties, even without prompting."
(rpgamer.com review)

**They interact with objects you place.** This is the signature mechanic: a
Marill rinses off under a shower you built; a Hitmonchan tries to sell you goods
at a cash register; Pokémon hop onto deckchairs for a snooze or mess with a
sandpit. Furniture isn't decoration — it's a behavior trigger.
(Sources: https://www.Cbr.Com/pokemon-pokopia-story-gameplay-review/,
https://wdcnews6.com/help-i-cant-stop-playing-pokemon-pokopia-and-its-all-thanks-to-one-very-clever-difference-from-animal-crossing/)

**They talk — constantly, funnily.** "It's rare for Pokémon to show personality
or even speak in the series, but the Pokémon in Pokopia are vocal and frequently
hilarious." They "speak their thoughts," are "perpetually curious about the
environment," and special dialogue occurs between certain Pokémon pairs.
Players report loving watching their "spirited conversations from afar" even
knowing it's randomized dialogue underneath.
(Sources: cbr.com review; rpgamer.com review; wdcnews6.com)

**Individual preferences, tracked in a codex.** Each Pokémon has detailed
preferences — favorite food flavors, décor tastes — recorded in the Pokédex.
Some want a new decoration, spicy food, or a darker place to sleep. This is a
discovery/collection layer on top of personality.
(Sources: rpgamer.com; cbr.com)

**Friendship progression deepens the bond.** As friendship rises, Pokémon start
calling the player by name, give gifts, and play mini-games. Named/story
Pokémon carry extra emotional weight.
(Source: https://rpgamer.com/review/pokemon-pokopia-review/)

**Real-time clock sync.** Day/night follows the Switch 2 system clock; crops
grow and Pokémon sleep on that cycle. Encounter/ambient behavior varies with
time of day and weather. Players famously feel guilty waking sleeping Pokémon
to put them to work — proof the attachment loop works.
(Sources: mygamingdiaries.com; https://www.thegamer.com/pokemon-pokopia-players-waking-up-pokemon-to-perform-work/)

## 5. Art style & animation

Pastel aesthetic with blocky, toy-like structures — described as "an adorable
mix of Minecraft and Animal Crossing" / Dragon Quest Builders lineage. The
protagonist Ditto-human is chibi. The softness of the look is repeatedly cited
as core to the cozy feel; one fan summarized the appeal as "the cuteness and
colourfulness."
(Source: https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2025/09/pokemon-pokopia-looks-like-an-adorable-mix-of-minecraft-and-animal-crossing)

## 6. Sound & music

Warm, nostalgic soundtrack (composers Hiromu Akaba and Jieun Kim, sound
supervision by Go Ichinose). 43 collectible Music CDs spanning nine generations
of Pokémon music, rearranged for the cozy setting, playable via crafted CD
players or DJ Rotom — an NPC Rotom possessing a stereo with its own exclusive
form/typing. Music is itself a collectible and a social object. A whole fan
culture of Pokopia lo-fi/ASMR mixes exists on YouTube.
(Source: https://thegameswiki.com/pokopia/wiki/soundtrack-and-music)

## 7. Design takeaways for our MBTI companion app

**T1 — Furniture as behavior triggers, not decoration.** Pokopia's most-loved
moments are Pokémon *using* things players built (shower, cash register,
deckchair). Map: our shared house's furniture should each unlock ambient
behaviors per type — the ISFJ "Warm Blanket" naps on any couch placed; the ENTP
"Mischievous Genius" fiddles with gadgets; the ESFP "Party Starter" gravitates
to the music corner. Every decor item purchased = new idle animations to
discover. This also monetizes naturally (furniture packs).

**T2 — Name them; friendship-meter the bond.** Pokopia's attachment spike comes
from *named individuals* whose friendship level unlocks name-calling, gifts, and
mini-games. Map: let users name their mascot; a visible bond meter unlocks
milestones — at level 3 it greets you by name in the morning, at level 5 it
"makes" you a gift (a doodle/shareable card), at level 10 a special in-type
confession dialogue. This is the cheapest retention mechanic in the genre.

**T3 — A "Field Guide" of type preferences.** Pokopia tracks each species'
favorite foods and décor tastes in the Pokédex — a discovery layer that makes
300 similar creatures feel distinct. Map: a codex of our 16 types' likes,
dislikes, ideal room items, and friendship tips ("Hype Machine loves confetti
poppers; wilts in silent rooms"). Collecting all 16 entries (via friends'
houses) becomes a completionist hook and a reason to visit other houses.

**T4 — Sync to the real clock; let time of day direct the mood.** Pokopia ties
sleep/wake and encounters to the system clock, which makes the world feel alive
for free. Map: our mood-bubble engine should be clock-driven first, AI second —
E-types get a morning energy spike, I-types get sleepy earlier, everyone naps
in the afternoon. The daily check-in then feels like catching a friend at a
real moment, and it drives the once-a-day open habit with zero content cost.

**T5 — Funny > deep for dialogue.** Pokopia's creatures are beloved for
randomized one-liners and pair conversations, and players adore it *knowing*
it's shallow. Map: we don't need LLM chat for v1 — a large pool of in-character
one-liners per type plus scripted two-mascot banter (INTJ + ESFP roommate
comedy) delivers ~80% of the charm at ~5% of the cost. This validates the
VISION.md "scripted first, LLM as premium later" call. Keep lines short,
meme-toned, and type-true.

**Bonus — design for guilt, not punishment.** Players feeling bad about waking
sleeping Pokémon is the strongest possible signal of attachment. Map: never
punish absence (no withering/death mechanics); instead reward return gently
("Pip saved you a seashell while you were gone"). Attachment without anxiety is
the cozy-game contract.
